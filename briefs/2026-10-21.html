High impact

The chip sell-off is concentrated in Asia's memory makers

Korea's Kospi fell 3.7% on Tuesday to 5,552.74, with SK hynix down 4.2% and Samsung down 3.0%. That came after a 7.6% crash on 15 October. Japan's Kioxia has also been hit hard. The reasons cited are fears of Chinese memory-chip competition, Samsung and SK hynix share buybacks ending, and doubts about who keeps paying for AI build-outs.

Why it matters: Korea, Taiwan and Japan's indexes lean heavily on chips, so this one theme drives most of the region. Korea is up about 2.7% in early Wednesday trading. Watch whether that bounce holds into the close.

High impact

Bond yields near 24-year highs keep pressure on the Fed and stocks

The 10-year Treasury yield is about 5.1%, just under its highest level since 2002. The Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023. Traders now see an 82% chance of no change at the 27–28 October meeting, but an 89% chance of another hike by December.

Why it matters: High yields make stocks look expensive, especially growth and tech, and they keep the dollar strong against Asian currencies. Any surprise from the Fed next week moves everything.

Medium impact

Guidance is moving stocks more than the earnings themselves

Companies that raised their outlook rose: 3M, Equifax (up almost 12%), Capital One (up about 4% after hours) and Super Micro (up about 14% after hours after lifting quarterly revenue guidance to $18.5–19.5B from $14.5–15.5B). Companies that beat but guided softly fell: GM dropped nearly 7% and Netflix fell about 5–6% after hours on a weaker Q4 revenue forecast and an ad-revenue cut to $2.7B from $3B. Intuitive Surgical fell about 7%.

Why it matters: Super Micro's jump shows AI server demand is still strong even as chip stocks fall. That is supportive for Taiwan's server and chip supply chain. For everything else, the market wants raised outlooks, not just beats.

Medium impact

US–Iran talks are the swing factor for oil

Oil eased on Tuesday after reports that mediators, working through Pakistan and Qatar, are trying to bring the US and Iran to talks. Signals are mixed: Secretary of State Rubio called a deal "extremely difficult", and Iran says the waterway it has closed stays shut until its conditions are met. Brent was trading around $110 earlier in the week.

Why it matters: Oil above $100 feeds inflation, which keeps the Fed hawkish and yields high. Japan, Korea and Taiwan import nearly all their oil, so a real breakthrough would be a clear plus for Asia.

Medium impact

SpaceX's first earnings beat lifts Nasdaq futures

In its first quarter as a listed company, SpaceX reported $14.6B in revenue against $12.87B expected, with $2.8B operating income. Shares rose about 7% after hours, and Nasdaq-100 futures climbed more than 1% overnight.

Why it matters: It gives tech a positive start to Wednesday's US session. Tesla reports after Wednesday's close, so the spotlight stays on Musk's companies.

Safe to ignore

  • "Tesla and SpaceX to merge." Both companies have only formed committees to study it. No terms exist, and a deal needs approval from 50% of all Tesla shares. Price targets like "$480 a share" are speculation.
  • Daily quotes from US and Iranian officials. Hopes for talks have come and gone many times. Wait for an actual meeting or for the waterway to reopen.
  • "Chip stocks are dead" takes. The sell-off is real, but some big banks still expect AI stocks to be much higher in 6–12 months. Treat both extremes with caution.
