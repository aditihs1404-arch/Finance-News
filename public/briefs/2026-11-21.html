High impactFri 20 Nov

Nasdaq sinks 5.3%; S&P 500 and Dow close in correction

The Nasdaq Composite lost 1,261.47 points, or 5.28%, to 22,617.73, turning negative for the year. The S&P 500 fell 3.70% to 6,910.42, its seventh straight decline, and the Dow dropped 1.93% to 48,147.33. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow ended more than 10% below their records, which is correction territory (CNBC, Yahoo Finance). Anthropic lost as much as 12% on its second day of trading, giving back most of its debut gain.

Why it matters: The AI sell-off has spread from a few chip stocks to the whole market. Rate-hike worries are still in the background, so there is little to cushion the fall.

High impactFri 20 Nov

Nvidia plunges 14% on report of SEC accounting probe

Reuters reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining "potential accounting issues" at Nvidia; the SEC declined to comment. The stock fell about 14% to a 52-week low, wiping out roughly $485–491 billion in value in one day (Yahoo Finance). CNBC says it is now on a seven-week losing streak, its longest since the 2008 financial crisis, coming after Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO. An investigation is not a finding of wrongdoing.

Why it matters: Nvidia is the biggest single weight in the AI trade. Accounting doubts hit confidence in the whole chip supply chain, from AMD and Micron to TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix.

High impact16–20 Nov

Chips post worst week since 2001; Asia faces Monday catch-up

Yahoo Finance says chip stocks had their worst week since 2001 and the Nasdaq-100 is nearing a bear market. In Asia on Friday, Taiwan's Taiex closed down 6.2% (down 17.5% on the week, its worst since 1991), the Kospi fell 5.2% and the Nikkei 225 about 5% — all before Nvidia's 14% drop in New York.

Why it matters: Taipei, Seoul and Tokyo have not yet traded on Friday's US losses. Expect more pressure on Monday morning, with foreign money already leaving Asian tech.

Medium impactFri 20 Nov

Yields fall sharply as investors run to bonds; gold slumps

The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 10–12 basis points to roughly 4.93–4.97%, back below 5% (CNBC). Gold did not act as a haven: futures fell about 4.6% to around $3,519, a fresh 52-week low. A December Fed rate hike is still almost fully priced in.

Why it matters: Lower yields ease pressure on borrowing costs, but they reflect fear, not good news. Gold's slide suggests investors are selling what they can to raise cash.

Medium impactFri 20 Nov

Oil heads for a third weekly drop as Hormuz flows recover

Late on Friday Brent traded around $92.2 and January WTI around $83.0, slightly higher on the day, but Brent was on track for a weekly fall of nearly 4% and December WTI about 5% (CNBC). Shipments through the Strait of Hormuz are back near pre-war levels, feeding expectations of a supply surplus.

Why it matters: Cheaper oil helps on inflation, which matters with a Fed hike on the table, and it helps big oil importers Japan, Korea and Taiwan.
