The one thing to know · updated Sat 07 Nov, 00:50 WIB

The AI trade melted down: the Nasdaq fell 5.5% into a correction, its worst day since April 2025, the S&P 500 lost 4.4% and the Dow almost 1,833 points, and Asia's chip markets plunged again on Friday.

  • Today: US stocks are rebounding after Thursday's rout, with the Nasdaq up about 1.4% to 1.9% mid-session, the S&P 500 up and the Dow down about 0.9%. Nvidia is up about 6%, the 10-year yield is near 4.87% and Brent is near $97.
  • Asia, Fri close: the Kospi ended near 5,060 after a 20-minute halt and an intraday drop of up to 8.8%. The Taiex pared an early 8%+ fall to close −4.5%, and the Nikkei, in a bear market, closed about 6% lower. Officials in Korea and Taiwan signalled support.
  • Rates: October payrolls rose 165,000 against about 59,000 expected, unemployment slipped to 4.1% and wages rose 0.4%. Futures price a more than 76% chance of a Fed rate hike on 9 Dec, so strong data now means higher yields rather than relief.

High impactThu 5 Nov

Nasdaq plunges 5.5% into correction in an AI-trade meltdown

The Dow closed at 51,523.06 (−3.44%), the S&P 500 at 7,353.73 (−4.36%) and the Nasdaq at 24,315.00 (−5.52%), more than 10% below last month's record. The Russell 2000 also entered a correction. Analysts tied the selling to fears that a Democratic Congress will slow the AI data-center build-out, and the Magnificent 7 lost about $1 trillion.

Why it matters: Wednesday's relief rally is gone, and with AI names carrying the index, the selling is spreading to the broader market.

High impactThu 5 Nov

Chip stocks crash: Nvidia and Qualcomm fall over 9%

The Philadelphia Semiconductor index dropped about 8.4% to its lowest level in more than six months. Nvidia, now negative for the year, and Qualcomm each fell more than 9%, and AMD, Micron and Applied Materials posted double-digit losses. Qualcomm's weak outlook, a memory shortage hitting smartphone sales, added to the pressure.

Why it matters: Chips lead the whole market, and Korea, Taiwan and Japan hold the memory and equipment suppliers in this chain.

High impactFri 6 Nov

Nikkei enters a bear market and the Kospi halts trading as Asia sells off

Japan's Nikkei 225 fell more than 20% below its June peak, with early losses reported above 6%. South Korea's Kospi plunged over 8% in early trade, triggering circuit breakers and a 20-minute halt, and the Kosdaq lost more than 7%. Figures are early-session and differ between reports.

Why it matters: SK Hynix, Samsung and Tokyo's chip and SoftBank-linked names are the Asian face of the AI trade, so the pain here feeds back into Nvidia and Nasdaq futures.

Medium impactFri 6 Nov

October jobs report today could reset Fed-hike bets

Economists expect nonfarm payrolls to rise about 59,000 after 29,000 in September, with unemployment at 4.2%. Job openings unexpectedly rose in September and private payrolls beat forecasts. Traders put the odds of a December rate increase at about 82%, per CME FedWatch data cited by Reuters. The 10-year yield held near 4.9% on Thursday.

Why it matters: A strong number would lift yields and hit already-battered growth stocks, while a weak one could ease hike fears.

Oil rebounds above $97 and gold edges up to $4,092

Brent climbed back above $97 a barrel after Wednesday's drop of more than 8% on hopes the Iran conflict would not escalate. Gold rose 0.46% to $4,091.80 an ounce, holding the previous day's jump as the dollar stayed near a one-month low. No dollar-index level was quoted.

Why it matters: Oil still drives inflation and rate expectations, and it matters for energy-importing Japan, Korea and Taiwan.
