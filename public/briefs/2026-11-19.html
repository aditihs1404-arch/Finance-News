High impactWed 18 Nov

Fed minutes: almost all officials see more hikes

Minutes of the October meeting, when the Fed held at 3.75–4%, showed almost all policymakers expect further increases if the economy develops as expected. Two said a move larger than a quarter point could be considered at the next meeting if inflation data disappoint. The S&P 500 fell for a fifth straight session, its longest losing streak since June, and stayed below its 200-day moving average.

Why it matters: A December hike is now almost fully priced (≈98%, CME FedWatch), and talk of a bigger move keeps pressure on stocks, gold and Asian currencies.

High impactWed 18 Nov

Nvidia slides 2.9% despite its blowout quarter

Nvidia closed at $150.96, down 2.89%. It was a fifth straight loss, and the stock is down more than 13% in a week. Revenue of $115.8 billion beat forecasts by more than $6 billion, but the new board chair said on the call that Jensen Huang will not return as CEO. Tighter US limits on Chinese access to chips also weighed. The fall dragged AI cloud names such as CoreWeave and Iren.

Why it matters: Even record numbers can't stop the selling. Leadership and China risk now drive the AI trade, which weighs on chip stocks in Seoul, Taipei and Tokyo.

High impact18–19 Nov

Anthropic prices record IPO at $83, trades today as "ANT"

Anthropic priced above its $70–78 range at $83 a share and raised the offering to about 980 million shares. That raises about $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX. The price values the company at about $1.5 trillion (CNBC; the price was reported by sources). Trading on Nasdaq starts in the US session tonight.

Why it matters: The debut tests how much appetite is left for AI stocks after Nvidia's slide. A big pop or a flop will move the whole AI group, and the deal could pull money from other AI names.

Medium impactWed 18 Nov

Gold at 14-month low, oil drops 4%, dollar at 19-month high

Spot gold fell about 2% to roughly $3,740, its lowest in 14 months, as the dollar climbed to a fresh 19-month high on hawkish Fed signals. WTI crude fell more than 4% to about $82.19 and Brent more than 2% to about $90.83. Longer-dated Treasury yields eased, with the 10-year near 5.06% and the 2-year at about 4.80%.

Why it matters: Cheaper oil helps the inflation outlook, but the strong dollar keeps the yen and won under pressure and hurts gold miners.
