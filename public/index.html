The one thing to know · updated Fri 30 Oct, 07:55 WIB

Alphabet's 12.6% plunge, its worst day since the 2004 IPO, pulled the Nasdaq down 1.37%, and Amazon and Intel falling after hours have Nasdaq-100 futures down about 1.7% and Asian chip stocks sliding.

  • Asia at the open (about 07:00 WIB): the Kospi fell about 3.1%, with SK hynix down about 6.4% and Samsung about 3.5%; Japan's Nikkei slipped about 0.7%, with SoftBank and Tokyo Electron down 4%–5%. Early moves, not closes.
  • Amazon's AWS grew 39% but the company missed on profit and sales, with free cash flow negative for a second quarter; shares fell about 4% in the session and roughly 6% after hours. Intel dropped about 21% on weak guidance.
  • Third-quarter GDP grew 3.4% (3.1% expected) and core PCE rose 0.3% as forecast, which sent the 10-year yield back to just below 5% but keeps rate-hike worries alive.

Morning brief · top stories, ranked by impact

Written Fri 30 Oct, 07:55 WIB. For anything newer, see "Since this morning" above.

High impactThu 29 Oct

Alphabet falls 12.6%, its worst day since the IPO, and drags the Nasdaq lower

Alphabet shed roughly $500 billion of market value after a revenue miss and heavier AI-related cash burn, and communication services led the market down. The Nasdaq lost 1.37%, the S&P 500 0.30% and the Dow 0.25%. The equal-weight S&P 500 rose more than 1%, so money rotated away from the biggest AI names rather than leaving stocks.

Why it matters: Investors are punishing AI spending that does not show up as cash flow. After Wednesday's three-day rally, the biggest index weights are now the weak spot, and Asian chip and tech stocks followed lower this morning.

High impactThu 29 Oct

Amazon and Intel slide after earnings; Nasdaq-100 futures fall about 1.7%

Amazon's AWS revenue rose 39% to $45.98B, above the $45.29B expected, and management said it expects AWS growth over 40% in 2027 and 2028. But EPS of $1.89 and revenue of $201.94B missed estimates, free cash flow was negative again and the $220B 2026 capex plan was unchanged. Amazon closed down 4.25% and after-hours reports ranged from about −6% to −9%. Intel plunged about 21% after hours on weak current-quarter guidance, which it tied to a PC slowdown.

Why it matters: Big Tech is being judged on cash flow, not just cloud growth, and Intel's guidance adds to doubts for chip names. Today's US open at 20:30 WIB is the test, and Korea and Taiwan chip supply chains are already reacting.

Medium impactThu 29 Oct

US GDP beats at 3.4%; core PCE in line; 10-year yield slips back below 5%

The first estimate of third-quarter GDP showed 3.4% annualized growth against 3.1% expected, and core PCE rose 0.3% on the month, as forecast and faster than August's 0.2%. The 10-year yield traded near 4.96%, down from Wednesday's 5.05% close, as tech weakness pulled money into bonds.

Why it matters: Strong growth with sticky inflation keeps the Fed's hike debate alive, and a 10-year yield near 5% weighs on growth stocks and on the yen and Asian currencies.

Medium impactThu 29 Oct

Caterpillar falls more than 9% despite beating estimates, weighing on the Dow

Caterpillar beat expectations but its construction industries sales were slightly below forecasts, and the stock was the biggest drag on the Dow, which closed down 0.25%.

Why it matters: It shows how little room there is for any shortfall in cyclical earnings with stocks near highs and yields near 5%. Machinery makers are a read on global industrial demand, including China.

Medium impactThu 29 Oct

Oil steady near $115 a barrel; gold slips

Brent traded around $115.40, little changed on the day, while spot gold fell about 0.3% to near $4,041 an ounce. The dollar index eased to about 101.9.

Why it matters: Oil above $100 feeds inflation and rate-hike worries and squeezes import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan, while energy stocks benefit.
