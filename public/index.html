High impact

AI-spending doubts hand the Nasdaq its worst day since June

The Nasdaq dropped 2.6% and the S&P 500 1.21%, its worst session since July, while the Dow edged up. Meta fell nearly 7% after its results and 2-for-1 split announcement, Tesla about 3% despite a beat, and Lam Research more than 11% on a soft outlook. Wednesday's SpaceX-led rebound did not survive.

Why it matters: The market is now punishing AI-linked names even on good numbers, which puts chip, memory and equipment stocks in Japan, Korea and Taiwan at risk at the open.

High impact

Oil jumps toward $111 on reports of US strikes on Iran

Brent traded above $110 and WTI near $98 to $99.50 after the New York Times reported the US and Israel plan renewed strikes on Iran after next month's midterms, and CNBC reported the US is pulling diplomats from Saudi Arabia. Settlement levels vary between reports.

Why it matters: Oil above $100 keeps inflation and Fed-hike worries alive and weighs on import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan. Energy shares and airlines are the direct movers.

Medium impact

Yields slip under 5% as jobless claims fall

The 10-year yield fell to about 5.04%, dipping below 5% for the first time since 23 September, and the 30-year to about 5.35%. Initial jobless claims were 194,000, below the roughly 200,000 expected, yet bonds rallied as stocks sold off.

Why it matters: Traders still see about a 90% chance of a December hike and only about 15% for the 27 to 28 Oct meeting, so relief on yields is flight to safety, not a changed Fed view.

Medium impact

Asia sells off Thursday, led by chips and tech

Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 1.6% to 63,595.58, Korea's Kospi 2.6% to 5,586.89 with Samsung Electronics down 3.9%, and Hong Kong's Hang Seng 2.7% to 23,003.17. Taiwan's Taiex fell roughly 0.8% to 1.2%. Worries that data-center building is getting costlier to finance added to the AI doubts.

Why it matters: Wall Street's Thursday drop landed after Asia had closed, so a second leg lower is possible at today's open.
