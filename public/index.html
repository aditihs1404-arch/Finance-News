High impactFri 30 Oct

Nasdaq falls 2.9% and S&P 500 1.8% as October ends with a tech sell-off

The Dow closed at 51,770.31 (−1.09%), the S&P 500 at 7,580.07 (−1.82%, its worst day since June) and the Nasdaq at 25,437.00 (−2.86%). Investors sold technology shares on AI-spending worries after Amazon and Intel results. For October the Nasdaq lost about 5.3%, its steepest month since March 2025; the Dow gained about 1.7%.

Why it matters: Money is rotating out of heavy AI spenders. Sunday-evening futures for the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 and Dow opened 0.2% to 0.4% higher, a modest bounce, so tonight's US open is the first real test.

High impactMon 2 Nov

Apple reports after the close, the headline of a 138-company earnings week

Apple reports after Monday's US close, in John Ternus' first call as CEO, with iPhone 18 sales in focus; CNBC's Jim Cramer notes the stock trades near 40 times earnings, leaving little room for error. Palantir also reports Monday and AMD on Tuesday; 138 S&P 500 companies report this week.

Why it matters: A big miss or cautious guide from Apple could extend the tech sell-off, with knock-on effects for Asian suppliers in Japan, Korea and Taiwan.

High impact3–6 Nov

Midterm elections Tuesday and the jobs report Friday, with December Fed-hike odds near 75%

Voters go to the polls Tue 3 Nov; Democrats are expected to win the House, with the Senate uncertain. The October jobs report lands Fri 6 Nov (economists expect roughly 55,000 to 75,000 jobs, unemployment 4.2%). The Fed held rates at last week's meeting, and CME FedWatch gives a quarter-point hike in December just under 75%.

Why it matters: With inflation well above the Fed's 2% target, a strong jobs print would strengthen hike bets and pressure growth stocks, gold and Asian currencies; investors are also watching what the vote means for AI data-centre spending.

Medium impactFri 30 Oct

Intel plunges nearly 29% to $65.76, its worst day since 1974

Intel guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15 billion to $16 billion, below the roughly $17 billion analysts expected, despite a third-quarter beat. CNBC also linked the drop to a plant shutdown in Israel and continued PC weakness.

Why it matters: It was the biggest single driver of Friday's chip weakness and a warning for PC-linked suppliers across Asia.

Medium impactFri 30 Oct

Treasury yields fall, gold hits its 2026 low below $3,900, oil stays high

The 10-year yield fell about 6 bp to 4.93% as bonds found a safe-haven bid during the tech rout; the 30-year was near 5.31%. Spot gold fell below $3,900, the lowest of 2026 and about 30% below its January peak, as higher oil and US inflation data fed Fed-hike bets. Brent was near $115 and WTI near $103 on Friday.

Why it matters: Oil above $100 feeds inflation and rate-hike worries and squeezes import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan, while lower yields did not help stocks on Friday.
