High impactMon 2 Nov

Oil plunges more than 7% as the US and Iran signal openness to diplomacy

WTI futures lost 7.4% to settle at $96.46 a barrel, the biggest daily drop since 27 July, and Brent fell 7.6% to $104.77. Analysts warn that rhetoric could heat up again after the midterms and push Brent back toward $120.

Why it matters: Lower oil eases inflation and Fed-hike worries and helps import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan, but the move can reverse quickly if talks stall.

High impactMon 2 Nov

Tech sell-off continues: Nasdaq −1.1%, S&P 500 −0.5%, while the Dow gains

The Dow closed at 51,983.98 (+0.41%), the S&P 500 at 7,541.40 and the Nasdaq at 25,164.32. The S&P gave up an early 1.1% gain as investors kept questioning heavy AI spending and rotated into defensive areas such as healthcare; about 70% of S&P 500 stocks still finished higher.

Why it matters: The AI-led weakness that hit the Nasdaq in October has not cleared, and it is what is pressuring Korean and Taiwanese chip makers this morning.

High impactMon 2 Nov

Apple beats on profit but drops after hours on a weak margin outlook

In John Ternus' first report as CEO, Apple posted revenue of $114.48 billion and EPS of $2.10 (about $0.05 from tariff refunds), against roughly $1.99 expected. It forecast rising memory costs for the December quarter, iPad sales missed, and the shares fell about 2% in extended trading. Palantir also beat, with revenue of $2.31 billion and a raised full-year outlook, though its shares dipped about 3% after hours.

Why it matters: Memory costs squeezing Apple's margin are good news for memory makers but show how heavy the AI-driven chip demand is on the supply chain; the reaction in Tokyo, Seoul and Taipei suppliers matters this week.

Midterms today and the jobs report Friday keep Fed-hike worries alive

Americans vote in the midterms today. The October jobs report is due Fri 6 Nov at 20:30 WIB. Weekend reports put December rate-hike odds near 75%, and the 10-year yield sits around 4.9%.
