High impactFri 6 Nov

Tech rebounds after the rout, but the week still ends in the red

The Nasdaq closed at 24,579.42 (+1.09%) and the S&P 500 at 7,376.86 (+0.31%), while the Dow slipped 237.33 points (−0.46%) to 51,285.73. Thursday's 5.5% Nasdaq plunge left the index in a correction, and the week finished with losses of about 3.4% for the Nasdaq, 2.7% for the S&P 500 and 0.9% for the Dow. The selloff has been tied to fears that new political winds in Congress will slow AI data-center spending.

Why it matters: One up day after a 5% drop is a bounce, not a bottom; the Nasdaq has now fallen four weeks in a row.

High impactFri 6 Nov

Hot jobs report puts a December Fed hike in focus

October payrolls rose 165,000 against forecasts near 59,000, and unemployment edged down to 4.1% from the expected 4.2%. The 10-year yield rose to about 4.86%. Reuters reports odds of a quarter-point hike at the 8–9 Dec meeting above 80%, though CNBC cited a lower figure of about 70% from before the report.

Why it matters: Good economic news now means higher yields rather than relief, which weighs on growth and AI stocks.

High impactFri 6 Nov

Asia's chip rout: Nikkei in a bear market, Kospi halted, Taiex down 4.5%

Japan's Nikkei 225 and South Korea's Kospi each closed about 6% lower, with the Kospi halted for 20 minutes. The Taiex recovered from an early fall of more than 8% to end down about 4.5% after officials in Taiwan and Korea signalled support. SK Hynix fell more than 11% and TSMC fell nearly 8.5% in the session.

Why it matters: These markets hold much of the AI supply chain. Monday's open shows whether Friday's US bounce reaches them.

Medium impactFri 6 Nov

Chips bounce after their worst day since 2020

The Philadelphia Semiconductor index fell about 12% on Thursday, its worst day since March 2020, then rallied about 4.2% on Friday. Nvidia, down 9% on Thursday, rose on Friday (gains of roughly 3% to 6% were reported at different points in the session). Microchip Technology tumbled about 14% on disappointing guidance.

Why it matters: Chips lead the whole market, and Korea, Taiwan and Japan supply many of them. Nvidia reports on 17 Nov.

Medium impact9–13 Nov

Week ahead: October CPI on Tuesday, plus Cisco, Applied Materials and CoreWeave

US CPI for October comes out on Tuesday 10 Nov. Reuters' poll expects a 0.3% monthly rise, while annual forecasts vary by source from about 3.7% to 3.9%. Cisco, Applied Materials and CoreWeave also report. Oil has fallen about 15% this week on hopes the Iran conflict will wind down, though tanker attacks and doubts about peace talks keep a floor under prices.

Why it matters: A hot CPI would reinforce the December hike bets, and chip-equipment results speak directly to the AI build-out debate.
