The one thing to know · updated Sat 31 Oct, 07:55 WIB

Wall Street ended October with a tech rout: the Nasdaq fell 2.9% on Friday and Intel dropped nearly 29%, its worst day since 1974, as AI-spending worries spread from Alphabet to Amazon and Intel.

  • The Dow lost 1.09%, the S&P 500 1.82% and the Nasdaq 2.86%; the Nasdaq fell about 5.3% in October, its worst month since March 2025.
  • Treasuries rallied (10-year ≈4.92%, about 40 bp lower in October) but gold slid below $4,000 on bets for another Fed rate hike in December (≈75% chance per CME FedWatch).
  • Next week: midterm elections Tue 3 Nov, Fed decision Wed 4 Nov and the US jobs report Fri 6 Nov. US trading resumes Mon 2 Nov at 21:30 WIB.

High impactFri 30 Oct

Nasdaq falls 2.9% and S&P 500 1.8% as October ends with a brutal tech sell-off

The Dow closed at 51,770.31 (−1.09%), the S&P 500 at 7,580.07 (−1.82%) and the Nasdaq at 25,437.00 (−2.86%). Investors sold big technology and semiconductor shares after Amazon and Intel results, extending Thursday's slide in Alphabet. The Nasdaq lost about 5.3% in October, its worst month since March 2025.

Why it matters: Markets are now punishing heavy AI spending that is not yet showing up as cash flow, and the pain is moving from one mega-cap to the next. Asian chip names in Korea, Taiwan and Japan fell sharply on Friday, and the next test is Monday's US open.

High impactFri 30 Oct

Intel plunges nearly 29% to $65.76, its worst day since 1974

Intel guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15 billion to $16 billion, below the roughly $17 billion analysts expected, despite a third-quarter beat. CNBC also linked the drop to a plant shutdown in Israel and continued PC weakness. The fall cut more than $11 billion off the US government's Intel stake.

Why it matters: It is the single biggest move behind Friday's chip weakness and a warning for PC-linked suppliers across Asia.

Medium impactFri 30 Oct

Amazon slips about 1.4% after a miss; $220 billion capex plan unchanged

Amazon missed on both revenue and earnings per share for the third quarter and kept its 2026 capital spending forecast at $220 billion. The stock fell as much as 5.9% during the day but closed down roughly 1.4% (some reports say nearer 2%).

Why it matters: Investors want to see returns on AI spending; AWS growth of 39% helped, but negative free cash flow keeps the debate alive.

Medium impactFri 30 Oct

Treasuries rally on the month, but gold hits its 2026 low on Fed hike bets

The 10-year yield fell about 7 bp to ≈4.92% on Friday, capping a rally of roughly 40 bp in October and the biggest monthly Treasury gain since 2024. Gold dropped about 3% to near $3,890, below $4,000 and the lowest level this year, as strong US inflation data and higher oil kept traders pricing about a 75% chance of a Fed hike in December.

Why it matters: Lower yields usually help growth stocks, but they did not on Friday, and expectations of more hikes weigh on gold and on the yen and Asian currencies.

Medium impactFri 30 Oct

Brent holds near $115, up about 12% in October, on signs the US may resume strikes on Iran

Brent settled near $115, a six-month high, and WTI rose toward $103. Yahoo Finance notes higher oil is also undercutting US stocks.

Why it matters: Oil above $100 feeds inflation and rate-hike worries and squeezes import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan, while energy stocks benefit.
