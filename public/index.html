Morning brief · top stories, ranked by impact

Written Sat 24 Oct, 06:20 WIB. For anything newer, see "Since this morning" above.

High impact

Oil hits a six-month high and the Dow drops 653 points

Brent settled at $116.72, up 3.1% and the highest settle since April, after reports that the US and Israel are drawing up plans for renewed strikes on Iran and an Iranian threat to hit US bases. WTI closed just above $103. The Dow fell 1.23% to 52,215.16; the S&P 500 slipped 0.52% and the Nasdaq 0.21%.

Why it matters: Oil above $100 keeps inflation and Fed-hike worries alive and hurts import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan. Energy shares gain; airlines and consumer names are the losers.

High impact

The 10-year sits at 5% with the Fed meeting next week

The 10-year yield rose about 5 basis points to roughly 5.00%, the 2-year to about 4.69% and the 30-year to about 5.31%. Markets expect no change at the Fed's Wednesday 28 Oct meeting but price about a 71% chance of a quarter-point hike in December.

Why it matters: Yields at 5% pressure growth and tech valuations, and a hawkish tone from the Fed on oil-driven inflation would hit the AI trade again. A firmer dollar also weighs on the yen and Asian currencies.

TreasuriesBanksGrowth / techUSD/JPY
Medium impact

Tech steadies after the AI rout, but worries linger

The Nasdaq nearly held flat after Thursday's 2.6% drop, helped by SpaceX gaining a rare double upgrade to Buy. Meta closed about 1% lower on top of Thursday's near-7% fall as investors fret about its first cash outflow in over a decade from AI spending. Bloom Energy fell on cash-flow concerns despite record third-quarter revenue.

Why it matters: The AI-spending debate decides the direction for chip and memory stocks in Asia, so Monday's open there will show whether the Thursday damage has stopped.

Medium impact

Asia steadies Friday after the Thursday rout

Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 0.3% to 62,693.16, Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.7% to 23,093.37 and the Shanghai Composite gained 0.9%. The Kospi closed up about 0.3% after an early drop of roughly 2.4%. Taiwan was closed for Typhoon Champi, so Taiex has not yet reacted to Thursday's US chip sell-off.

Why it matters: Taiwan reopens Monday with two days of US losses to price in, and Friday's Dow drop adds pressure on Asia's open.

Medium impact

Big Tech earnings arrive alongside the Fed

Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon and Intel are due to report next week, with ServiceNow on 28 Oct and Apple on 2 Nov (CNBC puts Apple's revenue expectation near $113 billion). Exact report dates for the first four were not confirmed in coverage found.

Why it matters: After Meta's reaction, investors will read capital-spending numbers closely; they set the tone for AI chip demand across Taiwan, Korea and Japan.
