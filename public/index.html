High impactThu 12 Nov

Wall Street absorbs Nvidia CEO exit; indexes dip about 0.4%

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO and director, with CFO Colette Kress named interim chief. Nvidia sank as much as 13% early but trimmed the drop to about 5%. The S&P 500 erased an early fall of nearly 1% before closing down about 0.35% at 7,592.40; the Dow lost 189.40 points to 52,297.09 and the Nasdaq about 0.39% to 25,366.09. AMD and Broadcom surged as money rotated to Nvidia's rivals.

Why it matters: The market's biggest stock lost its founder-CEO five days before earnings and indexes barely moved, a sign the AI trade is broader than one name; Nvidia's 17 Nov report is now the key test.

High impactThu 12 Nov

10-year yield jumps back above 5% as hike bets build

As Treasuries reopened after Veterans Day, the 10-year yield rose about 12–13 basis points to roughly 5.12% (CNBC), with 2-year and 30-year yields also higher. Higher oil and expectations of more Fed hikes drove the move; futures put the odds of a quarter-point hike at the 8–9 Dec meeting at about 90%. The dollar index touched 103.08, its highest since April 2025.

Why it matters: A 5%+ 10-year raises borrowing costs and squeezes stock valuations, especially tech; a firm dollar also weighs on Asian currencies, with the yen near 160.

High impactThu 12 Nov

Cisco beats and raises; says AI infrastructure revenue will double

Cisco earned an adjusted $1.40 a share on $18.5 billion revenue, against about $1.33 and $18.1 billion expected, and raised its fiscal-2027 adjusted EPS outlook to $5.47–$5.53 (consensus about $5.15). It said AI infrastructure revenue will double this fiscal year. Shares rose about 4% after hours. Applied Materials also beat ($4.16 vs ≈$4.05 a share; $10.41 billion revenue) and guided the January quarter to $10.6–11.6 billion, but its shares slipped after a 24% four-day run.

Why it matters: Strong AI networking and chip-equipment numbers support the AI spending story just as Nvidia's leadership is in question, and help explain why futures turned higher.

Medium impactThu 12 Nov

Oil jumps 2.6%, gold slides to its lowest in over a year

WTI settled up 2.55% at $88.74 and Brent up 3.3% at $95.82 after President Trump said the US doesn't need a deal with Iran. Gold fell about 2.7–3% to roughly $3,880 an ounce, its lowest in more than a year, on the strong dollar and rate-hike expectations.

Why it matters: Rising oil feeds the inflation worries behind the hike talk, while gold's slide shows how hard rising real rates are hitting non-yielding assets.
