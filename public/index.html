High impactWed 4 Nov

Dow surges 1,569 points, its best day since April 2025, after Democrats win Congress

The Dow closed at 53,356.12 (+3.03%), back above 53,000 for the first time since 8 Sept. The S&P 500 rose 1.58% to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq 1.44% to 25,736.51. Falling oil, easing yields and demand for healthcare and value-oriented retailers helped, and the rally beat JPMorgan's call for a 0.5% dip.

Why it matters: Political risk is easing and stocks are rotating away from AI names; one portfolio manager told CNBC the move could prove temporary.

High impactWed 4 Nov

Oil plunges more than 8% on hopes of an end to the Iran war

WTI futures fell 8.29% to settle at $88.12 a barrel and Brent fell 8.67% to $94.62. Analysts linked the drop to hopes for an end to the Iran war, after Vice President JD Vance blamed it for Republican election losses.

Why it matters: Cheaper oil lowers inflation worries and helps import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan, but it weighs on energy stocks.

Medium impactWed 4 Nov

Yields ease, dollar hits a one-month low, gold jumps 3.4%

The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 6 bp to roughly 4.89%. The dollar index slid to a one-month low. Front-month gold futures rose 3.4% to settle at $4,073 and silver futures gained 5.1% to $52.28, though both were among 2026's weakest assets before the midterms.

Why it matters: A weaker dollar and lower yields support gold and Asian currencies, and ease pressure on growth stocks.

High impactWed 4 Nov

After hours: AppLovin soars about 24%, Qualcomm drops about 6% on a weak outlook

AppLovin beat on results and guidance (adjusted EBITDA $1.84 billion vs $1.73 billion expected). Qualcomm beat on the quarter but its Q1 outlook fell short as a memory shortage weighs on smartphone sales. Coherent rose about 7%, while Expedia and Snap fell about 7% and Cava about 11%.

Why it matters: It sets the tone for ad-tech and chip stocks at Thursday's US open, and Qualcomm's supply warning touches the Asian handset and memory chain.

Medium impact5–6 Nov

Jobs report on Friday is the next test for Fed-hike bets

The Fed held rates last week, with two of its 12 voters backing a hike, and markets are weighing a possible December increase. A strong October payrolls report could raise those odds; weekly jobless claims come out Thursday.

Why it matters: A hot number could push yields back up and challenge the post-midterm rally in rate-sensitive growth stocks.
