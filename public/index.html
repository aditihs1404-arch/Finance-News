The one thing to know · updated Wed 04 Nov, 12:50 WIB

Early midterm results point to a Democratic sweep, and the AI-chip trade has flipped: Asian stocks fell sharply on Wednesday (Nikkei −3.6%, Kospi −3.2%, Taiex −2.3%) while Nasdaq futures dropped more than 2%.

  • Today: Asia sold off on the election and AI-regulation worries, with the Nikkei down 3.6% at 60,835 (lowest since early May), the Kospi down 3.2% at 5,467 after opening up about 3%, and the Taiex down 2.3%. S&P 500 futures were down about 1.2% and Nasdaq futures more than 2% in the overnight reports; these are snapshots, not closes.
  • Rates and oil: the 10-year yield fell nearly 5 bp to about 4.90% as investors bought Treasuries, the dollar index slipped 0.3% to about 101.4, and Brent eased to about $102.7 and WTI to about $95.1.
  • Ahead: Senate control is still open; US stocks reopen at 21:30 WIB tonight and the October jobs report is Fri 6 Nov at 20:30 WIB. JPMorgan had flagged only about −0.5% for the S&P 500 on a sweep, so futures are pricing a bigger hit.

High impactTue 3 Nov

AMD beats and guides far above forecasts; shares jump about 8% after hours

AMD earned $2.09 a share on revenue of $13.38 billion against $1.95 and $13.09 billion expected. Data center revenue rose 97% from a year earlier, and it guided to about $17 billion for the December quarter versus the $16.2 billion analysts expected. The stock had already risen about 8.7% in the regular session.

Why it matters: It is a strong answer to the AI-spending worries behind the Nasdaq-100's correction, and it is why Korean chip stocks bounced this morning.

High impactTue 3 Nov

Chips rebound on Election Day: S&P 500 +0.4%, Nasdaq +0.8%, Dow −0.4%

The S&P 500 closed at 7,568.92, the Nasdaq at 25,372.01 and the Dow at 51,786.72, with Apple dragging the Dow. Six of the 11 S&P sectors fell, led by materials and consumer staples. The Nasdaq-100 gained 0.7% to 28,180.26 but stays about 10% below its 6 Oct record.

Midterm results arrive with markets braced for a Democratic sweep

Polls pointed to a Democratic House and a closer Senate. S&P 500 futures were mixed and swung during the evening. Medium impactTue 3 Nov

Yields rise ahead of the jobs report; oil slips for a second day on Iran diplomacy hopes

The 10-year Treasury yield rose about 4 bp to roughly 4.93% and the 30-year to about 5.31%. WTI settled at $96.09 and Brent at $103.60 after dipping below $95 earlier, though US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island made trading choppy. Spot gold slipped about 0.5% to near $3,927 as the dollar and yields firmed.

Why it matters: Higher yields and a firm dollar pressure growth stocks, gold and Asian currencies; cheaper oil helps import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan.
