High impactMon 26 Oct

Anthropic's IPO filing lifts chips and the Nasdaq

Anthropic made its S-1 public, planning a Nasdaq listing as "ANT" and showing about $16.3 billion of first-half revenue. The Nasdaq rose 0.82% and the S&P 500 0.7%. By midday the semiconductor ETF was up nearly 3%, Nvidia 2.4%, Micron 3%, Broadcom 2.3% and AMD 2.8%. The share count and price range are not set yet.

Why it matters: It eased some worries about AI spending after two weak weeks, and the chip rally showed up in Asia early today, with the Kospi up about 3.8% and the Nikkei about 1.7%. A weak debut could later pull money away from AI stocks.

High impact27–29 Oct

The Fed meets with a hold expected and a December hike in play

The two-day meeting starts today, with the decision Wednesday at 2:00 pm ET (Thu 29 Oct, 01:00 WIB). Traders see about a 90% chance of a hold, and roughly 80% odds of a hike in December, per CME FedWatch via CNBC. The 10-year yield was little changed at about 4.99% and the 2-year about 4.72%.

Why it matters: Yields near 5% weigh on tech valuations. A hawkish tone on December would hit growth stocks and a firmer dollar would press the yen and Asian currencies.

High impact28–30 Oct

Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon test the AI spending story

Alphabet and Microsoft report Wednesday after the close and Amazon on Thursday, with roughly a third of S&P 500 companies reporting this week. Third-quarter GDP (expected near 3.2% growth) and PCE inflation both arrive Thursday at 8:30 am ET. Cloud growth and capital-spending guidance matter most.

Why it matters: A weak spending payoff would extend the tech sell-off and drag on chip and memory names in Asia, while strong cloud numbers would back the AI trade.

High impactMon 26 Oct

Oil slips on Iran diplomacy but stays near a six-month high

Brent settled at $115.81, down 0.8%, and WTI at $101.76, down 1.5%, as Qatari mediators went to Tehran. Attacks on Saudi Arabia and shipping disruptions keep traders wary, and US strategic reserves fell to 282.6 million barrels, the lowest since October 1982.

Why it matters: Oil above $100 feeds inflation and rate-hike bets, and it hurts import-dependent Japan, Korea and Taiwan. Energy stocks gain; airlines and consumer names lose.
