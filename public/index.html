The one thing to know · updated Thu 26 Nov, 00:50 WIB

The AI rout hit Wall Street: Nvidia fell 8% and the Nasdaq dropped as much as 4.6% before recovering to about −2% by midday, with firmer core inflation and new US tariffs piling on.

  • Today: by midday in New York (about 00:30 WIB) stocks were well off their lows: the Dow was down about 0.2%, the S&P 500 −1.2% and the Nasdaq −2.1%, after lows of −3.2% and −4.6%. Nvidia was still down about 8% and Micron about 10%. Treasurys rallied, with the 10-year yield down about 7 basis points to 4.82%.
  • October PCE inflation rose to 3.8% on the year, as expected, but core PCE was 3.3%, a tenth above forecast; Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3%. A December Fed hike is about 97% priced. Asia had already closed sharply lower: Kospi −5.6%, Nikkei −3.6%, Taiex about −4.8%.
  • The US proposed new Section 301 tariffs on 14 partners: about 20% on China and 15% on Japan, South Korea and the EU, with Taiwan also on the list. They are expected to start in January at the earliest; US markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving.

High impactTue 24 Nov

Chips give back Monday's surge; Nasdaq falls 1.5% while the Dow rises

The Nasdaq Composite closed down 1.5% at 23,733.54 and the S&P 500 fell 0.5% to about 7,201, while the Dow gained about 423 points, or 0.9%, to roughly 50,018 (Yahoo Finance). Nvidia fell about 6% after Monday's 31% jump, with Micron and AMD also sharply lower; investors rotated into value names such as Amgen, Nike and Salesforce.

Why it matters: One day after the Huang-driven rebound, the AI trade is fragile again. With Nvidia's board probe and reported SEC questions still open, chip stocks remain the swing factor for the whole market.

Asia opens lower: Kospi down about 4.5%, Nikkei about 1.4%

In early Wednesday trade (Tokyo and Seoul opened at 07:00 WIB), South Korea's Kospi fell 4.49% and Japan's Nikkei 225 1.41%, with Australia's ASX 200 down 0.41% (CNBC). US futures were slightly lower: Dow futures about −0.1%, S&P 500 futures −0.2% and Nasdaq-100 futures −0.3%.

High impactTue 24 Nov

Dell crushes estimates and raises its outlook by $10 billion

Dell earned an adjusted $9.45 a share on revenue of $54.94 billion, against LSEG estimates of $6.59 and $49.32 billion (CNBC), as AI server revenue surged. It raised its full-year revenue forecast by $10 billion and its adjusted EPS outlook to $29.68 from $25.50 (Yahoo Finance). Shares, which had fallen about 12% to $295.28 in the regular session, rose roughly 3–5% after hours.

Why it matters: After HPE, it is the second strong read on AI server demand this week, a counterweight to the chip sell-off. Whether it steadies Nvidia, Super Micro and Asian suppliers is today's question.

New US tariffs on dozens of countries are being prepared, China most exposed

The administration is readying duties on goods from dozens of trading partners, including China, Japan, South Korea and the EU; countries with trade deals are expected to be capped at 15% or lower, and final rates are not yet disclosed (WSJ via Yahoo Finance). Why it matters: Fresh tariffs add to inflation risk just as the Fed is set to hike, and they hit the Asian exporters on this page directly.
