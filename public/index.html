High impact

SpaceX's results ignite a tech and AI rally

SpaceX rose nearly 8% after reporting AI compute revenue up ninefold, and Super Micro's raised outlook added to the mood. The Nasdaq gained 1.05% and the S&P 500 notched a fourth straight gain. Communication services lagged, and AT&T fell 6.5% despite beating estimates.

Why it matters: It eases the chip-demand fears that drove Tuesday's rout in Korea and Japan, and Asia bounced the same day. The question is whether the rebound holds now that Meta and Tesla have reported.

High impact

Meta and Tesla beat, but after-hours reaction is split

Meta posted revenue of $64.6B against about $63.4B expected, EPS of $3.83 versus $3.45, guided above estimates and announced a 2-for-1 stock split; shares rose about 3% after hours. Tesla earned 48¢ adjusted on $28.05B revenue, both above forecasts, yet shares gave up earlier gains and traded down about 2%.

Why it matters: Meta's AI spending plans set the tone for chip and cloud names. Tesla's drop after a run-up on SpaceX merger talk shows how much good news was already priced in. Futures slipped on the mix.

Chip equipment stocks wobble on guidance

Lam Research dropped more than 5% after hours because its current-quarter outlook ran slightly under analyst expectations. IBM swung to a decline of more than 3% after an initial pop on its beat.

Why it matters: Lam's read on chip-equipment demand feeds directly into Tokyo Electron, Advantest and Korea's memory makers, which open in a few hours.

Medium impact

Bond yields stay near 5.1% ahead of the Fed

The 10-year Treasury yield sat around 5.12% and the 2-year near 4.79%, steady as investors waited on jobless claims and next week's Fed meeting. Traders put about an 83% chance on no change in October, but roughly 90% on another hike in December.

Why it matters: Yields this high cap how much stocks can re-rate, especially growth and tech, and keep the dollar firm against the yen and won.
