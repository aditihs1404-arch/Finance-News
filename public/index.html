High impactWed 25 Nov

Dow wipes out an 1,100-point loss as buyers rush back before Thanksgiving

The Dow closed up 164.25 points, or 0.33%, at 50,182.95, after being down more than 1,100 points at its low. The S&P 500 ended down 0.25% at 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq down 0.62% at 23,585.80, having been off as much as 3.2% and 4.6% (CNBC). Trading was light ahead of the holiday.

Why it matters: Dip-buyers showed up in force, but the market is fragile: intraday swings this large in a thin week mean the AI sell-off is not over, and Friday's half-day session gives little room to reset.

AI rout continues: Nvidia −4%, Micron and Sandisk −9 to −10%

Nvidia fell almost 4% at the close after being off nearly 7% at midday; CoreWeave was down about 6% midday and memory makers Micron and Sandisk lost about 9% and 10% (CNBC, Yahoo Finance). High impactWed 25 Nov

Sticky inflation: core PCE 3.3%, but bonds rally anyway

October PCE inflation rose 3.8% on the year and core PCE 3.3%, a tenth above forecast. Treasurys rallied regardless as investors bought bonds for protection, with the 10-year yield falling to about 4.82–4.87% (CNBC).

Why it matters: A December Fed hike stays almost fully priced, but falling yields on a hot print show bonds acting as a haven again, which helps cushion growth stocks.

High impactWed 25 Nov

New US tariffs on 14 partners hang over Asia's open

The White House unveiled proposed Section 301 tariffs on more than a dozen major trading partners, including China, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and the EU. CNBC said Asia-Pacific markets were set for a subdued open Thursday with investors weighing the duties; Nikkei futures were around 57,300 and Hang Seng futures 22,149, against closes of 57,011.62 and 21,966.01.

Why it matters: Exporters in Japan, Korea and Taiwan face higher costs on top of the chip sell-off, and tariffs add to US inflation risk with the Fed already set to hike.
