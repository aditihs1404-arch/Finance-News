High impactThu 19 Nov

Dow plunges 1,101 points, closes below 50,000

Stocks opened higher, then reversed hard once Anthropic began trading. The Dow fell 2.19% to 49,097.06, its first close under 50,000 since June. The S&P 500 lost 2.02% to 7,175.83, a sixth straight decline, and the Nasdaq fell 2.25% to 23,879.20. Nvidia, AMD and Alphabet were among the decliners. "The dam seemed to break once the big event was behind us," Interactive Brokers' Steve Sosnick said (Yahoo Finance).

Why it matters: The AI-led sell-off is deepening, not fading. A six-day losing streak with yields above 5% leaves little cushion, and chip-heavy Seoul, Taipei and Tokyo take the hit first.

High impactThu 19 Nov

Anthropic closes first day up 17% at $97.17

ANT opened at $111.50, 34% above its $83 IPO price, and touched $114.99 before fading to close at $97.17. CNBC put the closing value at about $1.8 trillion fully diluted (other estimates are lower). The IPO raised $81.3 billion, the largest on record. Mohamed El-Erian described the wider sell-off as investors "making room" for Anthropic (Yahoo Finance).

Why it matters: A solid debut, but money appears to be rotating out of other AI names to buy it. That weighs on Nvidia, AMD and Asian chipmakers. Alphabet and Amazon hold stakes in Anthropic, so its price now feeds into their valuations.

High impactFri 20 Nov

Asia opens sharply lower; Kospi down almost 4%

At the 07:00 WIB open, South Korea's Kospi fell 3.91%, Japan's Nikkei 225 1.46% and the Topix 1.29% (CNBC). Nikkei futures had pointed to about 57,890, against Thursday's close of 59,444.21. US stock futures were also slightly lower, with Nasdaq-100 futures down about 0.5%.

Why it matters: Korea is the most exposed to the AI chip trade through Samsung and SK Hynix. A third day of heavy selling in the region points to more foreign money leaving Asian tech.

Medium impactThu 19 Nov

Gold slides deeper; yields hold above 5%

Spot gold fell about 2% to roughly $3,665–3,676, a fresh 14-month low, as the dollar held near its highest since April 2025 (CNBC). The 10-year Treasury yield stayed around 5.05–5.09%. Markets priced about a 96% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike in December (CME FedWatch). WTI settled at $83.21, its lowest since late August, while Brent ended flat at $91.98 as Strait of Hormuz tanker disruptions offset surplus worries.

Why it matters: High rates and a strong dollar are squeezing every asset at once. That keeps pressure on the yen, the won and gold miners.

Medium impactThu 19 Nov

Walmart beats but slips; Palo Alto jumps on AI deal

Walmart earned an adjusted 65 cents a share on revenue of $188.84 billion, both above forecasts, and raised its full-year outlook; its shares still slipped early. Palo Alto Networks beat on revenue ($3.44 billion vs $3.31 billion expected), raised its fiscal 2027 forecasts and agreed to buy AI startup Applied Compute for $4.5 billion. Its shares rose about 10% early (CNBC).

Why it matters: Strong numbers aren't enough when valuations are stretched. Even so, consumer spending and cybersecurity demand are both holding up.
